# Saltwater Fish Pro — Design Direction

## Three stylistic approaches

### 1. The Marine Field Journal
**Very Brief Intro:** A light, editorial-first publication that combines the calm authority of a field journal with the visual depth of a healthy mixed reef. It is made for readers who want decisions explained with clarity rather than hyped with spectacle.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. The Glasshouse Gallery
**Very Brief Intro:** A gallery-like experience with oversized aquarium photography, warm stone surfaces, and restrained labels that make every system feel curated. It emphasizes aspiration, quiet luxury, and the aquarium as living design.

**Probability:** 0.04

### 3. The Technical Tide Chart
**Very Brief Intro:** A practical, diagram-forward workspace inspired by scientific field notes and maritime instruments. It puts diagnostic tools, system relationships, and data literacy in the foreground.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen approach — The Marine Field Journal

### Design Movement
Contemporary **editorial naturalism**: the considered, information-rich character of an independent publication paired with the immersive observation of a marine field guide. The site does not imitate an aquarium store or a crowded community forum.

### Core Principles
1. **System clarity over visual noise:** Every section should orient a reader toward a decision, a learning path, or an immediate next action.
2. **Editorial rhythm over generic grids:** Use asymmetric feature compositions, photo-led stories, quiet margins, rules, and layered content rather than repeated uniform cards.
3. **Aspirational but biologically credible:** Reef imagery must feel extraordinary without implying impossible livestock combinations, unrealistic equipment, or unsafe care practices.
4. **Trust made visible:** Reading time, category, review date, hierarchy, disclosure language, and careful typography should make the content feel accountable.

### Color Philosophy
The light **Salt White** canvas keeps long-form learning comfortable and lets vibrant reef photography carry emotional depth. **Deep Ocean Navy** provides authority and enclosure around the navigation, footer, and feature moments. **Reef Teal** and **Clearwater Aqua** are navigational signals rather than decorative noise, while **Coastal Sand** adds a restrained warmth that prevents the site from feeling cold or overly technical.

### Layout Paradigm
The site follows an **editorial current**: a wide image-led lead story flows into an off-axis reader-path strip, then transitions through a mix of large editorial spreads, narrow reference rails, and long reading columns. Content should feel discovered along a shoreline, not positioned in a rigid centered dashboard.

### Signature Elements
1. **The Stability Thread:** A thin, dotted or ruled teal line that connects the five system principles—Plan, Water, Equipment, Livestock, and Care—across key pages.
2. **Field-note labels:** Small uppercase metadata labels, review dates, and category tags treated as intentional editorial marks.
3. **Reef-window imagery:** Rounded but disciplined image frames with a subtle inset edge, like looking through exceptionally clean aquarium glass.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should reward curiosity without competing with reading. Cards lift minimally, image crops drift gently, and the Stability Thread can animate into view as content enters the viewport. Navigation should feel immediately responsive and never obscure the article’s purpose.

### Animation
Use a short custom ease-out for reveals, hover states, and buttons. Hero copy and visual anchors can enter in a subtle stagger; section elements should not slide dramatically or fade slowly. Animated content must respect reduced-motion preferences. The strongest motion is saved for the Stability Thread and photo-frame transitions, which should feel like a calm current rather than a technical effect.

### Typography System
Use **DM Serif Display** for page-level headlines, feature titles, and carefully selected pull quotes; it delivers the calm authority of an editorial journal. Use **Manrope** for navigation, body text, UI labels, and data-like metadata; its clean geometry supports long reading and technical clarity. Headlines are confident but not oversized; metadata is letter-spaced, compact, and precise.

### Brand Essence
**Positioning:** Saltwater Fish Pro is the stability-first editorial guide for people building and improving thoughtful mixed saltwater aquarium systems.

**Personality:** Composed, observant, exacting.

### Brand Voice
Headlines should be direct, specific, and outcome-led; CTAs should invite a next decision rather than demand conversion; microcopy should replace hype with useful context.

> “A beautiful tank begins with a stable one.”

> “Find the next right decision for your system.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The wordmark is a composed serif **Saltwater Fish Pro** set in DM Serif Display, accompanied by a custom monochrome reef-window mark: an offset rounded square containing a single continuous line that suggests a fish profile, a waterline, and branching coral. The mark must work alone as the favicon and is never reduced to a tiny, decorative afterthought.

### Signature Brand Color
**Reef Teal — `#0F6B72`** is the ownable signal color. It represents healthy water, measured action, and the link between field observation and system care.

## Style Decisions

- The default experience is light and editorial, not dark and immersive.
- Deep Ocean Navy is reserved for structural framing and high-impact feature moments.
- High-quality original visual assets should appear in the hero, feature-story, category, and newsletter areas; use distinct imagery in each position.
- The first release centers the editorial brand rather than a named founder.
- Every visual or layout decision should reinforce the Marine Field Journal philosophy; if it does not, remove it.
- Guide chapters open with an asymmetric reef-window observation before secondary navigation; they should never begin with a repeated dashboard-card grid.
- The dotted Reef Teal Stability Thread is the primary cross-page system motif and appears where Plan, Water, Equipment, Livestock, and Care are connected.
- The reef-window mark should remain a visible ownable element in the header, footer, and newsletter; the brand should not rely on typography alone.
